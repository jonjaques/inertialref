# Reconstruction without the velocity row: the presets re-measured

The temporal layout carries no `velocity` row: FSR reads the optics' masked
`motion.rg`, and the layout adds `reactive` alone (ADR-0044). These are
ADR-0044's held-preset frames, taken on package V's base, which still drew
the row, and on this branch, which does not, so the temporal figures stand on
the layout they describe.

- **Machine:** Mac mini (Mac18,5, Apple M6, 32 GB), macOS 27.2, Chrome
  157.0.8103.40 on WebGPU over Metal, Node 26.13.0. The display was asleep
  (`pmset displaysleepnow`) and nothing else built or measured.
- **Builds:** production previews (`pnpm preview`, port 8787), each from a
  clean tree. **Base** `bbea04da`, V's parent. **Tip** `23f173bc`,
  `weather/p1-cornerstones` with K and V merged, which holds V's two commits
  (`1aa2750e`, `5fc83578`); the page reports its build id in every row.
- **Rig:** `scripts/drive.mjs` at 1600 × 900 CSS pixels, DPR 1 and 2,
  `?chrome=0&layers=0`, no clouds flag.

## The protocol

One boot a preset and a DPR, so a tree is four boots and sixteen cells.
Each boot settles (`--settle`), pauses the world (`ir.pause()`), records the
renderer object and `ir.world.stateHash()`, and sets `ir.timing('off')`.
Each of the four pictures is then chosen **in the graphics panel**, as a
player chooses it: the page pushes `/settings/display` over the planetarium
as a dialog, clicks the anti-aliasing and render-scale options (standard
sharpness throughout), and goes back. The panel writes the preference, the
preference rebuilds the sensor in place, and the renderer is kept. Setting
`engine.picture` alone does not do it: the field changes and the sensor,
which rebuilds from the preference's React prop, does not, so every cell
reads the native layout. After a 2 s wait and a second settle, each cell
discards one `ir.gpu(60)` batch, takes five more and records the median,
then `ir.picture()`'s render size, `workingTextureBytes` and `initMs`, the
state hash and the renderer's identity.

Every one of the 32 cells settled, kept the renderer object, kept the
canonical state hash, left no dialog in the page and drew at the render size
its picture names. MSAA native is the in-boot control: it is the same
picture on both trees, so its difference is the rig's between two builds.

## What it says

- **Removing the row costs nothing and saves a little.** Every temporal cell
  is faster on the tip, by 0.008–0.167 ms; the largest savings are temporal
  native at DPR 2 (0.155 and 0.167 ms), where the row covered the most
  pixels. The MSAA control moved by −0.155 to +0.043 ms, so at Far Shore DPR 2
  the saving is inside the control's own difference, and the claim is
  "no cost", not a measured speedup there.
- **The working textures do not move,** because `workingTextureBytes` counts
  the upscaler's own allocations and the velocity row was a scene target.
- **`initMs` is the kernel's initialization** and varies boot to boot
  (temporal native at DPR 2: 112.9 and 124.1 ms on Tau Ceti Dusk, 218.5 and
  245.9 on Far Shore); it is not a figure about the layout.
- **On this machine temporal native costs more than MSAA on Tau Ceti Dusk**
  (5.137 against 4.485 ms at DPR 1, 15.955 against 14.033 at DPR 2) and less
  on Far Shore (6.290 against 7.147, 20.493 against 24.373). Temporal quality
  and performance are cheaper than MSAA on both presets at both ratios.

The rows are [`velocity-2026-11-10-base.json`](velocity-2026-11-10-base.json)
and [`velocity-2026-11-10-tip.json`](velocity-2026-11-10-tip.json), one object
a cell with its five batches.
