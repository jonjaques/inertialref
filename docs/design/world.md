# World

Setting, tone, and the diegetic frame that holds the real-data premise together.

> **This is a narrative-light game and this is a short page, on purpose.** There
> is no plot, no protagonist, no dialogue and no quest. What there is: a
> premise sturdy enough to explain every mechanic, and a tone consistent enough
> that the silence reads as intent rather than absence.

---

## The premise, in four sentences

Humanity has the Reference Drive and has had it for about sixty years. That is
long enough to have put outposts across a few hundred light-years and nowhere
near long enough to have looked at any of it properly. **The Survey** is the
distributed institution that maintains the catalog — the record of what is
actually out there — and it is chronically, structurally behind, because the
galaxy is large and there are not many people. You are a surveyor. Your job is to
go and look.

That is the whole setting. Everything else is a consequence.

---

## Why this premise

It is chosen to make the game's mechanics _true_ rather than to be interesting on
its own:

| Mechanic                                                     | The premise explains it                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Real astronomy is the catalog                              | The Survey's record is humanity's record, which is the real one |
| Procedural bodies are **projections**                        | The ship's computer models what should be there; it says so     |
| [Catalog revisions](galaxy.md#catalog-revisions)         | The Survey publishes. That is what it does.                     |
| [Discovery credit](exploration.md#discovery-credit)          | The record names who filed it, because records do               |
| [Data is the only currency](exploration.md#the-data-economy) | The institution pays in access, because that is what it has     |
| Inhabited space is tiny                                      | Sixty years is not long                                         |
| You are alone out there                                      | There are not many people and the galaxy is enormous            |

> 🎮 Designer's Note: The test for any addition to this setting is whether it
> makes a mechanic more true. Space pirates pass — someone wants your data.
> A galactic empire fails — it adds nothing and it makes the emptiness a lie.

## Tone

**Documentary, not operatic.** The register is a research vessel's log, not a
war. Nobody is chosen. Nothing is at stake beyond the specific, physical stakes
of a person a long way from help.

| It sounds like                                              | It does not sound like                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| _2001: A Space Odyssey_ — procedure, silence, scale         | _Star Wars_ — destiny, factions, opera      |
| _Solaris_ — the indifference of the thing you came to study | _Mass Effect_ — a galaxy that is about you  |
| _Outer Wilds_ — curiosity as the only motive                | _Destiny_ — power as the motive             |
| _The Expanse_ — physics as a source of tension              | _Star Trek_ — physics as a plot device      |
| Carl Sagan's _Cosmos_ — awe without sentiment               | Any narrator explaining how you should feel |

**Three tonal rules:**

1. **The universe is indifferent, not hostile.** Nothing in the galaxy is trying
   to kill you. Several things will, incidentally, if you are careless. That
   distinction is the difference between tension and threat, and it is why
   [on-foot combat is scarce](combat.md#on-foot-combat).
2. **Awe is earned by scale and silence, never announced.** No swelling score on
   arrival. No character saying "my god, it's beautiful." The gas giant filling
   the canopy is sufficient and anything added to it is subtraction.
3. **The institution is bureaucratic, not heroic.** Survey communications are
   dry, competent, and slightly overworked. That is funnier and more human than
   grandeur, and it sets off the enormity of what you are looking at.

## Voice

All text in the game is one of three registers, and they never mix:

| Register           | Used for                             | Example                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Instrument**     | HUD, gauges, readouts                | `THERMAL 84% · VENT ADVISED`                                                                                                        |
| **Record**         | Almanac, catalog panels, revisions | `b:3 · rocky · 1.09 M⊕ · thin CO₂ · surveyed 2026-09-14 · hyg-4.1`                                                                  |
| **Correspondence** | Survey messages, commissions         | _"Third request. The Anselm group still needs sub-2-Earth-mass confirmations and they are still not being fussy about which ones."_ |

Correspondence is the only place with a human voice, it arrives rarely, and it is
always text. **No voice acting anywhere**, which is a scope decision as much as a
tonal one — see [audio](audio.md).

## Naming

**Real objects keep their real names**, in full, with their designations. Alpha
Centauri is Alpha Centauri and also HIP 71683 and also GJ 559. That multiplicity
is real and it is part of the texture.

**Generated objects get a designation, not a name**: `HIP 71683 b:4`. A player
who first-discovers a body may name it, subject to a filter, and that name is
what everyone else then sees — which makes naming the most meaningful thing
[standing](progression.md#ratchet-3--standing) can offer.

**Resolved:** filtered free text online, with a report path for what gets
through; **no filter at all in solo modes**, because it is your universe. Naming
is the most meaningful thing [standing](progression.md#ratchet-3--standing) can
offer and it is worth the moderation burden — but that burden is real, it falls
on one person, and it scales with success rather than with effort.

## The one narrative surface

**Wrecks.** ⬜ Post-MVP.

The only authored content in the setting: a rare, scattered set of derelicts —
ships, failed outposts, abandoned installations — each with a small amount of
recoverable record. No quest chain, no arc, no reveal. Just the specific,
recoverable fact of what happened to a particular crew, found by someone who
happened to be there.

The reason this is the right narrative form for this game: it is **found, not
delivered**; it requires no NPCs, no dialogue system and no animation; it scales
by adding one more, forever; and its emotional register — someone was here and it
went badly — is the only story that fits a setting whose whole premise is that
the galaxy is enormous and the people in it are few.

**Resolved: recurring motifs, no resolution.** Patterns recur across wrecks — a
recurring cause, a recurring name, an instruction nobody explains — but there is
no answer and no ending. A mystery implies an answer, an answer implies an
ending, and this game should not have one. It rewards attention without ever
paying out, which is honest about what it is.

## What is deliberately absent

|                           | Why                                                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Plot, arc, ending         | The game is a place, not a story                                                            |
| NPCs to talk to           | No dialogue system, no animation budget, and nothing to say                                 |
| Factions at war           | Adds nothing to a survey game and makes the emptiness a lie                                 |
| Aliens                    | The single most expensive promise in science fiction. Not made, not hinted, not foreclosed. |
| Lore dumps, codex entries | The [Almanac](exploration.md#the-almanac) is the codex, and the player wrote it             |
| A narrator                | Rule 2                                                                                      |
